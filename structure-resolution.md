Disagreement is part of working together. Kani aims to address it early, fairly and without making people fear the loss of work, access or belonging.

Different concerns need different responses.

| Type of concern | Starting route |
|---|---|
| Creative or practical disagreement | The people directly responsible discuss it against the agreed brief and available resources. |
| Conflict over spaces, time, budgets or responsibilities | The relevant working-team members help clarify commitments and develop an agreement. |
| Unresolved disagreement affecting Kani’s direction or policy | The council considers it through a facilitated discussion. |
| Concern about conduct, harassment, discrimination, retaliation or misuse of authority | A confidential route involving an impartial contact; it is not treated as an ordinary programme disagreement. |

## Resolving a working disagreement

First, clarify what is disputed: the goal, decision authority, resources, behaviour or an unmet commitment.

The people involved identify what they need and explore workable options. A colleague can facilitate if everyone is comfortable with that arrangement.

If the matter remains unresolved, it goes to the appropriate working-team lead or council. A person directly involved in the dispute does not act as its impartial facilitator.

Record any agreement, who will act and when it will be reviewed.

## Raising a sensitive concern

Kani names a confidential contact and an alternative contact outside the immediate working relationship. People can approach the alternative directly when the concern involves their usual contact, a founder, a council member or someone with authority over their work.

No one is required to confront the person concerned before seeking support. Mediation is voluntary and is not assumed to be suitable for serious misconduct.

Before information is shared, explain who needs access and why. Sensitive complaints do not go into public meeting minutes.

The process should provide:

- Prompt acknowledgement and a clear next step.
- Practical support and protection against retaliation.
- An impartial assessment, with external assistance where needed.
- A fair opportunity for the people concerned to be heard.
- A reasoned outcome and an independent route to request review.

People can use appropriate external support or complaint routes without first exhausting Kani’s internal process.

## Conflicts of interest and accountability

Anyone connected to a supplier, applicant, partner or disputed decision declares that interest and steps aside where necessary.

Concerns about a council member’s conduct or repeated failure to fulfil their role follow a documented review involving non-conflicted people. The member has an opportunity to respond. Any replacement follows the selection process for that seat.

Each review should also ask whether unclear responsibilities, unrealistic workloads or poor access to information contributed to the problem, and what Kani needs to change.
