Research, Residencies & Learning gives people time, space, relationships and resources through which to investigate questions, develop practices and share knowledge.

Work may begin with artistic practice, ecology, craft, technology, lived experience or local knowledge. It may result in publications, exhibitions, prototypes, performances, gatherings or new forms of learning.

## How does the work happen?

| Pathway | Purpose |
|---|---|
| **Research** | Investigate questions through artistic, ecological, technological, material and community knowledge. |
| **Residencies** | Give artists, researchers, makers and other practitioners supported time to develop their work in relation to Kani’s place and communities. |
| **Learning** | Exchange knowledge through mentorship, workshops, field schools, demonstrations and collaborative practice. |

A programme may combine all three pathways. A residency might begin with research, involve a local learning exchange and conclude with an exhibition or publication.

## What can be explored?

Areas of enquiry may include:

- Water, climate, local ecologies and speculative futures
- Craft, materials, making and sustainable production
- Creative technology and its cultural and social implications
- Oral histories, living knowledge and community-led archives
- Reflective practice, wellbeing and personal growth
- Transdisciplinary collaborations that connect different forms of knowledge

These are areas of enquiry, not permanent departments. Appropriate practitioners, knowledge holders and specialists are invited according to the needs of each programme.

Heart programmes such as Nurupa may use residential, mentored or hybrid formats to connect self-enquiry, creative practice and community learning. See [Heart & Wellness](#heart-wellness).

## What should each programme put in place?

Every research, residency or learning programme should make clear:

- The central question or field of enquiry
- Who is participating and who is shaping the work
- The time, space, mentorship, equipment and financial support available
- How local and specialist knowledge will be credited and compensated
- What access, care and consent are required
- What may be documented, shared or kept private
- What should remain after the programme

The selection of individual artists, residents, fellows or contributors follows the approved programme’s own brief and criteria. See [Programme Selection](#programme-selection).

## What can the work produce?

The work may produce an exhibition, performance, publication, prototype, archive, workshop, public conversation or further research. Not every programme needs a public presentation or a finished object.

Kani Publications and other platforms may carry research into public life, but the enquiry should not be forced into an outcome that does not serve the work.

## How do we know the work is meaningful?

We ask whether the programme:

- Creates space for a significant question to be explored
- Supports genuine exchange between disciplines and forms of knowledge
- Treats participants and knowledge holders fairly
- Deepens understanding of a place, practice or subject
- Produces learning that can be carried forward
- Opens a useful next step, even when the original enquiry remains unresolved
