Kani’s programme framework helps us understand what kinds of work Kani undertakes, how different activities connect and what deserves to continue over time.

It has three connected elements:

- **Pillars** describe the purpose of the work.
- **Programmes** are the activities and projects Kani develops.
- **Platforms** are named, continuing structures that can hold multiple programmes, projects and editions.

## What kinds of work does Kani undertake?

Kani’s work is organised through three pillars:

| Pillar | Purpose |
|---|---|
| **Research, Residencies & Learning** | Develop knowledge, creative practices, skills and new forms of enquiry. |
| **Creative Experiences** | Bring people together through art, culture, ideas and making. |
| **Commercial Experiences & Space Hire** | Generate income through commissioned experiences, hospitality and the use of Kani’s spaces. |

The pillars help us understand the primary purpose of a programme.

## What holds this work together?

Heart and grace form the foundation of all three pillars.

Community participation, experimentation, ecological responsibility, transdisciplinary exchange, continuity of knowledge and sustainable operation shape how programmes are developed and delivered.

These principles apply whether the programme is a residency, festival, publication, gathering, workshop, marketplace, meal or commercial engagement.

## What is the difference between a pillar, a programme and a platform?

- **Pillars** describe why the work exists.
- **Programmes** are the actual activities and projects being undertaken.
- **Platforms** are recognisable, continuing structures through which related programmes can develop over time.

A platform may hold multiple editions, projects, gatherings, collaborations and outputs.

For example, Creative Experiences is a pillar. Kanifest is a platform. A particular edition of Kanifest is a programme developed within that platform.

Platforms draw on Kani’s shared Working Team, spaces, infrastructure and programme-specific appointments.

Formats such as exhibitions, workshops, residencies, meals, performances and publications do not automatically become platforms. They are ways in which programmes can take form.

## Can a programme belong to more than one pillar?

Yes. Transdisciplinary programmes can move between research, public exchange and commissioned or income-generating activity.

A residency might bring an artisan, technologist and local knowledge holder together to explore a question. Their research could become an exhibition, workshop or publication. An institution might later commission a related learning experience.

The same enquiry can move across the pillars without losing its original purpose.

## Can a platform work across more than one pillar?

Yes. A platform can hold different kinds of programmes.

Kani Publications may document research, produce an artist publication and undertake a commissioned editorial project. Kani Canteen and Food Lab may research local ingredients, host community meals and develop paid food experiences.

This movement across the pillars is central to Kani’s transdisciplinary approach.

## What are the current platforms?

| Platform | What it holds |
|---|---|
| **Kanifest** | An interdisciplinary festival developed through distinct editions, curatorial propositions and community participation. |
| **Kani Publications** | Editorial projects, research outputs, field notes, books and other forms of documented knowledge. |
| **Curated Marketplaces** | Recurring encounters connecting artisans, makers, demonstrations, workshops and cultural programming. |
| **Sunset Baithaks** | Intimate sunset gatherings around music, poetry, performance, conversation and collective listening. |
| **Kani Canteen and Food Lab** | A space for food experimentation, shared meals, local ingredients, culinary knowledge and feeding the community. |

Other platforms may emerge as Kani’s work develops. Not every workshop, exhibition, residency, gathering or event needs to become a platform.

## How does Kani decide what to programme?

A proposed programme should have:

- A clear relationship to Kani’s ethos and one or more pillars
- Relevance to a community, audience or field of enquiry
- A named creative owner and delivery owner
- Realistic time, resources and funding
- An approach to participation, access and care
- A plan for documentation, reflection or continued learning

Programmes can begin as small experiments. They may be repeated, revised, expanded or allowed to conclude. See [Programme Selection](#programme-selection) for the complete process.

## When does a programme become a platform?

A programme can develop into a platform when it has:

- A clear purpose and recognisable identity
- A reason to continue beyond a single event or project
- The capacity to hold multiple programmes, editions or collaborations
- A named owner
- A realistic resource and funding model
- A way to carry relationships, documentation and learning forward

Becoming a platform is not automatically a measure of success. Some of Kani’s most meaningful programmes may remain temporary, experimental or specific to a particular place and moment.
