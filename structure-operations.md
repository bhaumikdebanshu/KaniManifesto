Kani maintains shared systems and services so programmes can draw on dependable support without recreating the same infrastructure each time.

## What shared functions are required?

| Function | Who carries the work | Main responsibilities |
|---|---|---|
| **Programme pipeline** | Programme Coordinator | Proposal records, review status, approved briefs, review points and programme learning |
| **Programme calendar** | Programme and Operations Coordinators | Dates, capacity, dependencies and connections between activities |
| **Spaces and equipment** | Operations Coordinator with technical support | Bookings, readiness, maintenance, inventory, operators and changeovers |
| **Institutional partnerships and funding** | Partnerships lead | Relationships, proposals, agreements and reporting, supported by relevant advisors |
| **Commercial enquiries** | Named commercial or booking owner | Enquiries, qualification, quotations, visits and follow-up |
| **Accounts** | Accounts team | Costing, financial records, invoices, payments and understandable budget reports |
| **Communications** | Design & Communications | Confirmed information, announcements, registrations and audience access information |
| **Hospitality** | Hospitality team | Accommodation, arrivals, food and guest support within agreed capacity |
| **Community relationships and care** | Community & Care with programme leads | Participation, access, feedback, welcome and continuing relationships |
| **Technical support** | Internal or appointed specialists | Sound, projection, digital systems, fabrication, installation and equipment operation |

A function may initially be carried within another paid role, but every responsibility needs a named owner.

## How are institutional partnerships managed?

Advisors, Council members, founders and programme leads may open relationships. The Partnerships lead owns organisational follow-through.

For each relationship:

- A named person maintains contact
- The purpose and possible programme are recorded
- Programme leads shape the content
- Operations checks capacity
- Accounts confirms costs and reporting requirements
- Community & Care contributes where participation or representation is involved
- An authorised person makes the final contractual commitment

An introduction does not become a Kani commitment until it passes through this process.

## How are commercial enquiries handled?

Commercial enquiries may involve:

- Space hire
- Hospitality and stays
- Retreats and offsites
- Facilitated creative experiences
- Commissioned publications or cultural projects
- Programme design and curation for another space

The commercial or booking owner receives and qualifies the enquiry.

The relevant people then determine:

- What the client is purchasing
- Whether the work is consistent with Kani’s ethos
- Whether Kani is advising, co-producing or delivering
- Who holds creative and curatorial authority
- What staff, space, equipment and relationships are required
- Whether the price recovers the complete cost
- Whether the work displaces existing commitments
- What contract, payment and reporting arrangements are needed

For a programme designed for another space, a named creative or curatorial lead owns the content. Partnerships holds the client relationship, Operations checks feasibility, Accounts supports costing and an authorised person signs the agreement.

## How are budgets managed?

The Council agrees annual priorities and overall allocations, informed by available funding and team capacity.

Every programme has:

- An approved budget
- A named budget holder
- Clear spending authority and limits
- A view of committed, available and spent funds
- An agreed approach to changes and contingencies
- Any required financial and narrative reporting

Routine expenditure proceeds within agreed limits. Transfers affecting another programme or shared resource require consultation and the appropriate approval.

Approval, payment and financial recording should involve distinct people wherever possible.

Commercial work accounts for its full demands on Kani, including staff time, utilities, setup, maintenance, hospitality and recovery time.

## How are spaces and shared resources allocated?

One calendar and resource plan covers:

- Public programmes
- Residencies and learning activities
- Festivals and platforms
- Commercial bookings
- Guest stays
- Maintenance and technical work
- Setup, changeover and recovery time

A confirmed booking or programme includes its complete use of the place—not only the hours visible to participants.

Public-interest and community programmes receive protected time. Commercial work should support Kani’s continuity without gradually taking over its calendar.

## What records and systems are maintained?

Kani maintains proportionate records for:

- Programme proposals and decisions
- Appointments and mandates
- Budgets and financial commitments
- Contracts and permissions
- Spaces and equipment
- Institutional and community relationships
- Consent, access and care requirements
- Documentation and programme learning
- Concerns and follow-up where appropriate

Personal contact details and sensitive information are kept in suitable internal systems with appropriate access and consent.

Records should support continuity and accountability without turning people or relationships into administrative data alone.

## How do enabling entities relate to operations?

The responsibilities of SoUF, KiLab and M/s Kani Home—including contracts, payments, employment, property and authorised signatures—must be documented alongside the Council and Working Team mandates.

Everyone should be able to understand:

- Which entity is undertaking an agreement
- Who may sign or authorise it
- Which budget and account apply
- Who is operationally responsible
- What reporting or legal obligations follow

Entity authority should be transparent. It should not become an informal route for overriding agreed programme or governance decisions.
