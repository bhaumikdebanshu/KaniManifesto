**Current proposal—for discussion.** Decision rules, financial limits and entity delegations need collective agreement before use.

Decisions should be made as close as possible to the people responsible for the work.

| Decision | Who decides |
|---|---|
| Routine work within an agreed role | The responsible person, consulting affected colleagues. |
| Programme content within an approved brief | The relevant curator or programme lead. |
| Delivery arrangements within approved resources | The producer or delivery lead. |
| Changes affecting another team or shared resources | The affected leads agree together, coordinated by the working team. |
| Annual priorities, overall allocations and major changes of direction | The council. |
| Contracts, payments and other reserved commitments | The relevant authorised person or entity, following the agreed decision and delegation. |

Advisors, founders and partners use these routes rather than giving parallel instructions to team members.

## Reaching agreement

For a collective decision:

1. Explain the proposal, its purpose, resources and consequences.
2. Hear the people affected and clarify questions.
3. Revise the proposal where concerns reveal a better approach.
4. Check agreement explicitly.
5. Record the decision, responsible person and review date.

Consensus means reaching a proposal everyone involved can support or live with. It does not require everyone to prefer the same solution.

For practical, reversible changes, the group can agree a time-limited trial. The question is whether there is a significant reason the proposal should not be tried within those limits.

Silence is not agreement. A person may accept a proposal with reservations, which are recorded. Agreeing not to block a proposal does not commit someone to unagreed work.

## Council decisions

The proposed quorum is five of seven members, including at least one working-team representative, one community representative and one external practitioner.

Council decisions require explicit agreement among the eligible members participating. Members receive proposals in advance and can raise concerns even when unable to attend.

If agreement cannot be reached, revise the proposal, seek facilitation, agree a smaller trial or defer it. The Convenor cannot break a deadlock alone.

Members with a conflict step aside from the affected decision. If there are too few eligible members to meet quorum, the matter waits for a properly constituted decision.

## Urgent situations

Programme plans identify who can pause an activity or take immediate action to protect people, the premises or essential services. Any emergency spending authority has a stated limit.

The person acting records what happened and reports promptly. Urgency does not create a general right to bypass agreed responsibilities.
