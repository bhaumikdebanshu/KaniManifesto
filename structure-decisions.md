Decision rules, financial limits and entity delegations must be collectively agreed before they become operating authority.

Decisions should be made as close as possible to the people responsible for the work. Decentralisation depends on clear mandates, not the absence of accountability.

## Who decides what?

| Decision | Who decides |
|---|---|
| Routine work within an agreed role | The responsible person, consulting affected colleagues |
| Develop or pilot a programme idea | The temporary programme review group convened for that proposal |
| Proceed with a programme within agreed priorities and resources | The relevant non-conflicted reviewers, with the decision and conditions recorded |
| Programme content within an approved brief | The appointed curator, creative lead or programme lead |
| Delivery arrangements within approved resources | The producer or delivery lead |
| Selection of programme participants or contributors | The people named in the approved programme brief |
| Routine commercial booking within agreed pricing and capacity | The booking owner and Operations Coordinator |
| Commissioned programme or programme design for another space | The relevant creative lead, Partnerships, Operations and Accounts, followed by required contractual authorisation |
| Change affecting another team or shared resource | The affected leads together, coordinated by the Working Team |
| Establish a new platform | The Council, informed by evidence from earlier programmes |
| Annual priorities, overall allocations and major changes of direction | The Council |
| Contracts, payments and other reserved commitments | The relevant authorised person or entity following the agreed decision |

Advisors, founders, partners and funders use these routes rather than giving parallel instructions.

See [Programme Selection](#programme-selection) for how programme proposals are reviewed.

## What is a temporary programme review group?

A review group is assembled for a particular proposal. It is not another standing committee.

It normally includes:

- The Programme Coordinator
- The relevant creative, research or programme lead
- The Operations Coordinator
- Community & Care when participation, access or representation is significant
- Partnerships and Accounts when funding, pricing, contracts or reporting are involved
- Relevant practitioners, knowledge holders or advisors where specialist judgement is needed

The group should be no larger than the proposal requires. The people developing an idea may explain and discuss it, but no proposal should be approved by its originator alone.

## How is agreement reached?

For a collective decision:

1. Explain the proposal, its purpose, resources and consequences.
2. Hear the people affected and clarify questions.
3. Identify conflicts of interest.
4. Revise the proposal where concerns reveal a better approach.
5. Check agreement explicitly.
6. Record the decision, conditions, responsible people and review date.

Consensus means reaching a proposal everyone involved can support or live with. It does not require everyone to prefer the same option.

Silence is not agreement. A person may accept a proposal with reservations, which are recorded. Agreeing not to block a proposal does not commit someone to work they have not accepted.

For practical and reversible decisions, the group may agree to a time-limited trial.

## When does a decision go to the Council?

A matter goes to the Council when it:

- Changes Kani’s purpose, direction or public commitments
- Establishes or concludes a continuing platform
- Requires a major or unallocated use of resources
- Creates a long-term institutional obligation
- Affects the organisation’s governance or legal responsibilities
- Cannot be resolved within existing mandates
- Presents a significant ethical, financial or reputational risk

A proposal is not escalated merely because it is creatively ambitious or because one participant prefers a Council decision.

## How does the Council decide?

Council quorum is two-thirds of the appointed membership, rounded up.

This means:

- Five members when the Council has seven
- Six members when the Council has eight
- Six members when the Council has nine

The quorum must include:

- At least one Working Team representative
- At least one community or youth representative
- At least one founding or enabling-entity representative
- At least one independent representative when independent seats have been appointed

Council members receive proposals in advance and may raise concerns when unable to attend.

Council decisions require explicit agreement among eligible participating members. The Convenor has no casting vote.

If agreement cannot be reached, the Council may:

- Revise the proposal
- Seek additional information or facilitation
- Approve a smaller time-limited trial
- Defer the matter
- Decide not to proceed

Members with a conflict step aside. If too few eligible members remain to meet quorum, the decision waits for a properly constituted meeting.

## How are decisions recorded?

The record states:

- What was decided
- Why the decision was made
- Who participated
- Any declared conflicts
- Conditions or limits
- Who is responsible for the next step
- When the decision will be reviewed

The level of detail should match the importance of the decision. Sensitive personal information is not included in public records.

## What happens in an urgent situation?

Programme and operational plans identify who may pause an activity or take immediate action to protect people, the premises or essential services.

Emergency spending authority has a stated limit. The person acting records what happened and reports promptly.

Urgency does not create a general right to bypass agreed responsibilities.
