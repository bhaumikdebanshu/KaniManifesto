Kani Home brings together creative practice, learning, community and enterprise. Its structure should help people take responsibility, work together and care for the place without concentrating every decision in one person.

## What is the structure?

Kani works through four connected parts:

| Part | Main responsibility |
|---|---|
| **Advisory Circle** | Contributes specialist knowledge, mentoring, critical review and relationships without becoming another management layer |
| **Kani Council** | Holds Kani’s shared direction, agrees priorities and overall resource allocations, and remains accountable for how the organisation works |
| **Working Team** | Maintains the year-round programmes, relationships, spaces and systems that make the work possible |
| **Programme Teams** | Develop and deliver particular programmes within an agreed purpose, budget, period and mandate |

The Advisory Circle strengthens the work with knowledge and relationships. The Council provides direction and clear mandates. The Working Team maintains continuity. Programme Teams gather around particular work and hold authority within their approved brief.

The Advisory Circle appears first because it brings a wide field of knowledge into Kani. It does not sit above the Council in authority. Advisors contribute; the Council remains responsible for organisational decisions.

## How does a programme move through the structure?

1. An idea enters through the Working Team, a community member, advisor, institution, practitioner, client or earlier programme.
2. The Programme Coordinator helps identify its primary purpose, Kani’s role and the people needed to review it.
3. A temporary review group considers its purpose, participation, capacity, finances and pathway-specific requirements.
4. The idea may be developed, piloted, approved, revised, held or declined.
5. Once approved, it receives a brief, budget, authority, review point and the Programme Team it needs.
6. After delivery, its value, relationships, finances, workload and learning are reviewed.

The Council becomes involved only when a proposal changes Kani’s direction, requires a major allocation, establishes a new platform or creates commitments beyond the Working Team’s mandate.

## Where does authority sit?

Authority follows responsibility.

- A curator or creative lead makes content decisions within an agreed brief.
- A producer or delivery lead organises delivery within agreed resources.
- Continuing Working Team members make routine decisions within their mandates.
- Appointment groups select people for defined roles.
- The Council decides matters affecting Kani as a whole.
- Authorised people and enabling entities complete contracts, payments and other reserved commitments.

Advisors, founders, partners and funders use these routes rather than giving parallel instructions to team members.

Every programme and continuing function has a named owner. Shared governance should make responsibility easier to understand, not make ownership disappear.

## What does the starting structure look like?

The proposed starting structure is:

- An Advisory Circle covering six areas of contribution
- A seven-member Council, with the option to expand to nine when a clear representation gap is identified
- Four continuing Working Team roles
- Named paid capacity for partnerships and community care
- Existing Accounts and Hospitality teams
- Funded Programme Teams appointed according to need

Responsibilities may be combined when skills, time and compensation allow. Additional work should not be assumed to happen informally or without support.

Council seats are governance responsibilities rather than staff positions. Advisors and Council members may contribute without holding paid employment, but honoraria, travel and access support should be provided where possible.

The structure should be reviewed after six months. New continuing roles or Council seats are added only when there is a clear need and sufficient capacity to support them.

## What holds the structure together?

**Whatever we do, we do with heart and grace.**

This shapes how people are welcomed, how authority is exercised, how work is distributed, how disagreements are handled and how contributions are recognised.

Care is a shared responsibility. It includes realistic workloads, fair compensation, accessible participation, respect for consent and a safe route for raising concerns.
