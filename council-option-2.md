## Heart — the shared foundation

Everything we build begins with heart and grace. We approach people, places and practices with care, attention and respect. This shapes how we welcome, listen, collaborate, disagree and support one another.

This proposed foundation sits beneath all three pillars. [Heart & Wellness](#heart-wellness) can also develop year-round mindfulness, reflection and wellbeing activities across the pillars, alongside care for the working team. Programme details remain open for collective development.

## Proposed council composition

Four perspectives come together in one council. The groups describe contributions, not separate departments or ranks. Members can contribute beyond their category.

| Group | Seats | Proposed roles and contribution |
|---|---|---|
| Practice & Systems Advisors | 5 | One each for Arts, Craft & Cultural Practice; Research, Learning & Publishing; Design, Systems & Technology; Business & Enterprise; Fundraising & Partnerships. Build capability, mentor the team and develop practitioner, institutional, industry and funding relationships. |
| Programme members — ex officio | 5 | Festival Curator; Arts & New Media Curator; Sound & Music Curator; Film & Moving Image Curator; Craft & Material Practices Curator. Bring current practice, practitioner relationships and programme experience. |
| Community representatives | 2 | Selected through a process developed with local residents, practitioners and participants. Bring priorities, access needs and lived experience; gather views and report back. |
| Youth representatives | 2 | Young people selected through an accessible youth-led nomination or selection process. Help shape opportunities, learning and Kani’s future direction. |
| **Total council seats** | **14** | The Director attends as operational lead, additional to these seats. Other working-team members join relevant items. |

Start with one advisor per area. Invite further specialists for particular enquiries rather than automatically adding standing seats. Community and youth seats are distinct places; one person does not fill two seats.

## Advisors and curators

An advisor supports the wider organisation across programmes and years. A curator develops a particular programme within an agreed brief. For example, the Arts, Craft & Cultural Practice Advisor helps build mentoring and institutional relationships; the Arts & New Media Curator develops the festival track.

Ex officio means membership by virtue of holding a role. Each serving curator joins through their appointment and leaves that seat when the appointment ends. These seats are additional to the advisors. An unfilled curatorial role leaves a vacant seat; no appointment is made solely to fill the council.

Advisors and community/youth representatives serve one-year terms, reviewed annually and renewable. Each has an agreed remit, time commitment and participation or compensation arrangement. Curatorial appointments include time for council work and handover.

Members step out of discussions concerning their own appointment, fees or performance. Interests in commissions, selections or partnerships are declared and handled through an agreed conflict process.

## Council remit and working authority

The council collectively shapes annual priorities, the balance between programmes, participation and access, and how Kani’s work reflects its ethos. It reviews experience and recommends what to continue, change or stop. It advises on major partnerships, new platforms and long-term development.

Before adopting the model, specify which matters the council decides and which are recommendations requiring approval elsewhere. The council’s title alone creates no financial, contractual or employment authority.

| Responsibility | Proposed owner |
|---|---|
| Shared direction, participation priorities and programme learning | Council, informed by community gatherings and the team; exact decision authority to be agreed. |
| Realistic programme, staffing and resource plans; everyday coordination | Director within delegated authority, reporting progress and difficulties to the council. |
| Artistic and editorial choices, schedules and delivery within approved briefs | Appointed curators, Producer, editor and relevant programme leads. Routine work does not await council approval. |
| Budgets, contracts and delegated authority | Authorised representatives of the relevant entity. |
| Appointment and review of the Director | An arrangement to be agreed between the enabling entities, with a named accountable route. |

Industry introductions and sponsorship support are valued alongside mentoring, local knowledge and care. The working team develops proposals and follows up on relationships. Membership does not guarantee sponsorship or confer control over programme selections.

## Participation beyond the seats

Hold a quarterly open community gathering before the council meeting. Community and youth representatives bring its priorities to the council and report back on responses. Offer ways to contribute without attending a meeting. One person is not expected to represent every local perspective.

Community and youth members help prepare agendas, receive the same information and have supported opportunities to influence recommendations. Budget for honoraria, travel and access needs; agree accessible languages and meeting times.

When resources allow, pilot a small community-directed programme fund. Agree the amount, eligible activities, decision process and practical boundaries in advance. Participants help choose activities; the team supports delivery; the next gathering reviews the experience. This is a proposal, not a funded commitment.

## A light working rhythm

- Appoint one Convenor annually from the council. Prepare agendas with the Director and community/youth representatives, support balanced participation and follow up on actions.
- Meet as a full council quarterly. Allocate staff time for light secretariat support.
- Advisors agree practical contributions between meetings, such as mentoring, introductions, proposal review or developing a system with the team.
- Form small working groups for defined questions, with relevant community and youth participation, an owner and an end date.
- Record recommendations or decisions, reasons, action owners and review dates. Report back while keeping personal or sensitive concerns out of public records.

## Before choosing an option

- [ ] Discuss which model fits Kani’s purpose, capacity and participation budget.
- [ ] Confirm seats and whether festival representation adequately connects to year-round research, learning, publishing and other programmes.
- [ ] Develop community and youth selection, eligibility, renewal and reporting-back arrangements with the people involved.
- [ ] Confirm authority, relationships with the enabling entities and accountability for the Director.
- [ ] Agree decision-making, quorum, voting if used, vacancies, recusals and the Director’s participation rights. Protect meaningful community and youth influence.
- [ ] Allocate convening, administration, honoraria and access support before appointments.
- [ ] Review the model after one year: who participated, whose proposals shaped decisions, what was delivered and what coordination cost.

Choosing Option 2 would require coordinated changes to council, decision rights and related team and programme pages. Until then, retain both options as proposals for comparison.
