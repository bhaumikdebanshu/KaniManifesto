This process helps Kani choose what to develop, support, host or undertake with a partner. It leaves room for experimentation, community proposals and unexpected collaborations while making decisions understandable and accountable.

Not every idea needs to begin as a complete proposal, and not every opportunity needs to become a competitive application.

## What does this process select?

It selects the programmes Kani will commit its name, time, relationships, spaces or resources to.

These may include:

- Research projects, residencies and learning programmes
- Exhibitions, workshops, gatherings and performances
- Publications, meals and public experiences
- Commercial bookings and commissioned experiences
- Programmes designed or curated for another space
- Collaborations developed with communities, institutions or funders

The selection of individual artists, residents, fellows, facilitators, contributors or vendors happens after the programme itself has been approved.

## How can an idea enter?

An idea may come from:

- The Working Team or a Programme Team
- Community members and local practitioners
- The Advisory Circle or Council
- An artist, researcher, institution or potential partner
- A client or commissioning organisation
- An open call, invitation or commissioned opportunity
- Learning from an earlier programme

No route gives an idea automatic priority. Invited, commissioned and partner-supported proposals still require an appropriate review.

## What kind of proposal is it?

Before reviewing an idea, Kani identifies:

- Its primary pillar
- Any secondary pillar it connects to
- Whether Kani is the initiator, host, partner, commissioned curator or delivery team
- Whether it will happen at Kani Home, elsewhere or across both
- Whether it is a one-time programme or sits within an existing platform

A programme may connect to more than one pillar, but naming its primary purpose helps Kani decide how it should be reviewed.

A programme does not need to be identified as a new platform at this stage. Platforms emerge only when there is a clear reason and capacity for work to continue over time.

## What information is needed?

An idea begins with a short programme note that explains:

- The central idea, enquiry or intended experience
- Its relationship to Kani’s ethos and pillars
- Kani’s proposed role
- Who it is for and who should help shape it
- The proposed creative owner and delivery owner
- Likely activities, outputs and timeline
- Where the programme will take place
- Space, people, equipment and partnership needs
- An initial budget and possible source of funds
- Participation, access, care and ecological considerations
- Who will hold the relevant relationships
- What should be documented, learned or carried forward

For commissioned work, the note should also identify the client, what they are purchasing and whether Kani is advising, co-producing or delivering the programme.

Early ideas do not need a complete proposal. The note should provide enough information to decide whether further development is worthwhile.

## Who reviews an idea?

The **Programme Coordinator** holds the process and brings together the people required for the review.

- The relevant programme or creative lead considers purpose, quality and programme fit.
- The **Operations Coordinator** checks space, timing, staffing, equipment and technical capacity.
- **Community & Care** contributes when participation, access or continuing relationships are important.
- **Partnerships and Accounts** contribute where funding, contracts, pricing or reporting are involved.
- Relevant community members, practitioners or knowledge holders are included when the work affects, represents or draws knowledge from them.
- Advisors may contribute specialist knowledge, critical review or relationships.
- The Council considers proposals only when they change Kani’s direction, require a major allocation or create commitments beyond the Working Team’s mandate.

The people who develop an idea may join the discussion, but the decision and its reasons should not rest with one person alone.

## What questions apply to every proposal?

| Question | What to consider |
|---|---|
| **Does it belong at Kani?** | Its relationship to Kani’s ethos, pillars, place and longer direction |
| **Who finds it meaningful?** | Its relevance to participants, communities, practitioners, audiences or a field of enquiry |
| **Who should shape it?** | Whose knowledge, experience or participation is needed during development |
| **Does it invite genuine exchange?** | Its transdisciplinary possibilities and the quality of participation it creates |
| **Can it be cared for well?** | Access, consent, wellbeing, fair compensation, representation and realistic workloads |
| **Can it be delivered?** | Time, people, space, equipment, permissions, partnerships and operational complexity |
| **Can it be sustained financially?** | Budget, funding route, earned income where appropriate and the complete cost to Kani |
| **What will remain?** | Learning, relationships, documentation, public value or a reason to continue |

These questions support judgement; they are not a mechanical score. A small local experiment may be more valuable than a larger, better-funded proposal.

## What additional questions depend on the kind of work?

| Pathway | Additional questions |
|---|---|
| **Research, Residencies & Learning** | Is there a meaningful enquiry? What time, mentorship, space and financial support are required? How will knowledge be credited? Is a public outcome appropriate? |
| **Creative Experiences** | How will people encounter or shape the work? Is it accessible? Does the proposed format serve the idea? What kind of relationship should continue afterwards? |
| **Commercial Experiences & Space Hire** | What is the client purchasing? Does the price recover the full cost? Will the work contribute to Kani’s sustainability without compromising its ethos or displacing its own programmes? |
| **Programme design for another space** | Is Kani advising, co-producing or delivering? Who holds curatorial authority? Who approves the programme? Who selects, contracts and pays contributors? |
| **Community or partner-supported work** | Were relevant people involved early enough? Are community and institutional relationships being treated distinctly? Who is responsible for commitments and follow-up? |

A programme that moves across pillars may need questions from more than one pathway.

## How are people selected?

Programme approval comes before the selection of individual participants or contributors.

Each approved programme defines:

- The people or skills it needs
- Whether selection will happen through an open call, invitation, nomination, commission or procurement process
- The criteria that will be used
- Who will make the decision
- What payment, support and working conditions are available
- How conflicts of interest will be handled
- How applicants or invitees will be informed

Not every opportunity requires an open call. A direct invitation may be appropriate when a programme requires a particular practice, relationship or form of knowledge. The reason for the invitation should still be recorded and understandable.

## What decisions can be made?

| Decision | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Develop** | The idea is promising and receives time to prepare a fuller brief |
| **Pilot** | Test the idea at a limited scale before making a larger commitment |
| **Proceed** | Confirm the brief, owners, budget, authority and review point |
| **Revise** | Return with specific changes to the scope, method, relationships or resources |
| **Hold** | Keep the idea for a more suitable time, capacity or funding opportunity |
| **Decline** | Do not proceed, with a short and respectful reason |

Every decision identifies who will communicate the outcome and what happens next.

## How are fairness and accountability protected?

Anyone involved in a decision declares personal, financial or professional conflicts. A conflicted person may provide factual context but does not make the final decision.

Selection criteria and decision-makers are stated in advance for open calls and paid opportunities. Applicants receive clear information about the process, timeline, available support and expected commitments. Sensitive personal information remains private.

Kani keeps a short internal decision record containing:

- The proposal
- The people consulted
- The decision and its reasons
- Any conditions that must be met
- The people responsible for the next steps
- The agreed review point

Public communication may use an appropriate summary.

## What happens after selection?

Before work begins, every programme receives an agreed brief, named owners, budget, authority, schedule and review point. Selection is not permission to exceed these boundaries.

Depending on the kind of work, the programme may also require:

- A participant-support and mentorship plan
- A participation, access and outreach plan
- A documentation, consent and knowledge-crediting agreement
- A commercial scope, full-cost budget and client contract
- An agreement defining curatorial authority and production responsibility
- A process for selecting and contracting contributors

After delivery, the team reviews the programme’s value, participation, workload, finances, relationships and learning.

The programme may conclude, be repeated, be revised or—when there is a clear purpose, identity and capacity to continue—develop into a platform.
