This process helps Kani choose what to develop while leaving room for experimentation, community proposals and unexpected collaborations. It should make decisions understandable without turning every idea into a competitive application.

## What does this process select?

It selects the programmes Kani will develop, support or host. These may include residencies, exhibitions, workshops, gatherings, research projects, publications, meals, commissioned experiences and other forms of work.

The selection of individual artists, residents, fellows, contributors or vendors happens within the approved programme’s own brief. That process must state its criteria, decision-makers, support and conflicts of interest.

## How can an idea enter?

An idea may come from:

- The Working Team or a Programme Team
- Community members and local practitioners
- The Advisory Circle or Council
- An artist, researcher, institution or potential partner
- An open call, invitation or commissioned opportunity
- Learning from an earlier programme

No route gives an idea automatic priority. Invited and partner-supported proposals still need an alignment and capacity review.

## What information is needed?

Begin with a short programme note that explains:

- The central idea or enquiry
- Its relationship to Kani’s ethos and pillars
- Who it is for and who should help shape it
- The proposed creative owner and delivery owner
- Likely activities, outputs and timeline
- Space, people, equipment and partnership needs
- An initial budget and possible source of funds
- Participation, access, care and ecological considerations
- What should be documented or learned

Early ideas do not need a complete proposal. The note should be detailed enough to decide whether further development is worthwhile.

## Who reviews an idea?

The **Programme Coordinator** holds the process and brings together the people needed for the review.

- The relevant creative lead considers purpose, quality and programme fit.
- The Operations Coordinator checks space, timing, staffing and technical capacity.
- Partnerships and Accounts contribute where funding, contracts or reporting are involved.
- Community members or relevant practitioners are included when the work affects or represents them.
- Advisors may contribute specialist knowledge or relationships.
- The Council considers only proposals that change Kani’s direction, require a major allocation or create commitments beyond the team’s mandate.

The people who develop an idea can join the discussion, but the decision and its reasons should not rest with one person alone.

## What questions guide selection?

| Question | What to consider |
|---|---|
| **Does it belong at Kani?** | Connection to the ethos, pillars, place and longer direction. |
| **Who finds it meaningful?** | Relevance to communities, participants, practitioners or a field of enquiry. |
| **Does it invite genuine exchange?** | Participation, transdisciplinary possibility and whose knowledge shapes the work. |
| **Can it be cared for well?** | Access, consent, wellbeing, fair compensation and realistic workloads. |
| **Can it be delivered?** | Time, people, space, equipment, permissions and operational complexity. |
| **Can it be sustained financially?** | Budget, funding route, earned income where appropriate and the full cost to Kani. |
| **What will remain?** | Learning, relationships, documentation, public value or a reason to continue. |

These questions support judgement; they are not a mechanical score. A small local experiment may be more valuable than a larger, better-funded proposal.

## What decisions can be made?

| Decision | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Develop** | The idea is promising and receives time to prepare a fuller brief. |
| **Pilot** | Test the idea at a limited scale before making a larger commitment. |
| **Proceed** | Confirm the brief, owners, budget, authority and review point. |
| **Revise** | Return with specific changes to scope, method, partnerships or resources. |
| **Hold** | Keep the idea for a more suitable time or funding opportunity. |
| **Decline** | Do not proceed, with a short and respectful reason. |

Every decision identifies who communicates the outcome and what happens next.

## How are fairness and accountability protected?

Anyone involved in a decision declares personal, financial or professional conflicts. A conflicted person can contribute factual context but does not make the final decision.

Selection criteria and decision-makers are stated in advance for open calls and paid opportunities. Applicants receive clear information about the process, timeline, available support and expected commitments. Sensitive personal information remains private.

Kani keeps a short internal decision record: the proposal, people consulted, decision, reasons, conditions and review point. Public communication can use an appropriate summary.

## What happens after selection?

Before work begins, the programme receives an agreed brief, named owners, budget, authority, schedule and review point. Selection is not permission to exceed these boundaries.

After delivery, the team reviews value, participation, workload, finances, relationships and learning. The programme may conclude, be repeated, be revised or—when there is a clear reason and capacity to continue—develop into a platform.
