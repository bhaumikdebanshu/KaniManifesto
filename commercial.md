Commercial Experiences & Space Hire generate earned income through Kani’s spaces, hospitality and creative capabilities.

This work helps sustain Kani’s people, infrastructure and public-interest programmes while remaining consistent with its ethos.

## How does Kani generate earned income?

| Model | What it may include |
|---|---|
| **Use Kani’s spaces** | Gallery hire, screenings, presentations, meetings, private events and short-term workspace |
| **Stay and gather at Kani** | Retreats, offsites, guest stays, hospitality and residential team programmes |
| **Commission Kani’s practice** | Creative-learning experiences, publications, workshops and tailored cultural projects developed by Kani |
| **Design a programme for your space** | Curatorial concepts, programme strategy, contributor selection, partnerships, schedules and delivery frameworks developed for another space or institution |

Kani may design a complete programme for a cultural space, school, institution, workplace, hospitality venue or other public-facing environment. This could involve understanding the place and its audiences, developing a curatorial direction, identifying artists or practitioners, building partnerships and creating a plan for delivery.

The programme may be delivered by the commissioning organisation, produced jointly with Kani or managed by Kani as an additional service.

A commercial engagement may also combine programme design with the use of Kani’s spaces, accommodation, food, facilitation or creative production.

## What makes an engagement commercial?

An engagement is primarily commercial when a client purchases:

- The use of a space or facility
- Hospitality or accommodation
- A facilitated experience
- A commissioned programme, publication or creative outcome
- Curatorial or programme-design expertise for another space

Charging for a public workshop, festival or cultural programme does not automatically make it commercial.

Similarly, a partner-funded programme may remain a research or creative programme when its primary purpose is public, educational or community-facing.

## What principles guide commercial work?

Commercial engagements should:

- Recover the full cost of delivery
- Contribute to Kani’s shared infrastructure and long-term sustainability
- Pay people fairly and account for staff time
- Remain consistent with Kani’s ecological, cultural and community commitments
- Avoid regularly displacing Kani’s own programmes
- Protect appropriate curatorial and editorial independence
- Use community relationships and cultural knowledge responsibly
- Make clear what Kani is providing and what the client is purchasing

When Kani designs or curates a programme for another organisation, the agreement should clarify who holds final curatorial authority, who selects and contracts contributors, and who is responsible for production and delivery.

Commercial activity should support Kani’s purpose rather than gradually redefine it.

## What must be agreed before proceeding?

Every commercial engagement requires:

- A defined scope and intended outcome
- A named relationship owner and delivery owner
- A realistic budget, price and payment schedule
- An account of staff, space, equipment and hospitality costs
- A written agreement covering responsibilities, permissions and cancellations
- A confirmed place in the shared calendar
- A review of operational, financial and reputational risks
- Clarity about documentation, publicity and the use of Kani’s name

For programmes developed for another space, the agreement should also define:

- The curatorial brief and intended audience
- Who approves the programme
- Who appoints and pays participating practitioners
- Whether Kani is advising, co-producing or delivering the programme
- How the programme and Kani’s role will be credited

See [Operations & Resources](#structure-operations) for delivery responsibilities and institutional partnerships.

## How do we know the work is successful?

We ask whether the engagement:

- Created appropriate value for the client or partner
- Paid for its complete use of Kani’s resources
- Contributed financially to Kani’s continuity
- Was delivered without unreasonable pressure on people or infrastructure
- Strengthened rather than compromised Kani’s reputation and relationships
- Created a worthwhile reason to work together again
