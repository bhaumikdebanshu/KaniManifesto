**Status: proposed model for discussion.** Membership, appointments, decision rules and financial delegations require collective agreement before adoption. The earlier governance and staffing chapters remain reference proposals; this chapter sets out Option 3 in full.

## Executive summary

Kani Home is proposed as an organisation governed through **shared stewardship**. Authority is distributed across a council, a small working group and programme teams, with decisions made close to the work.

A **16-member Shared Stewardship Council** brings together six advisors, three programme delegates, one Heart delegate, two working-team delegates, two community representatives and two youth representatives. It agrees direction, annual priorities, shared policies and resource-allocation principles, and reviews how Kani is working.

A **Working Coordination Group** carries four responsibilities: Creative Practice & Learning; Community & Care; Partnerships & Institutional Development; and Systems, Operations & Resources. These are responsibilities, not four director posts. The group's size follows the actual appointments.

**Year 1 centres on two continuing paid coordination roles: Programme Coordinator and Operations Coordinator.** Design & Communications works alongside them. Community & Care has allocated time within the team or part-time support; Partnerships can begin through a part-time or retained appointment. Existing accounts and hospitality staff support delivery. Each responsibility has a named owner, realistic capacity and appropriate compensation.

Programme teams make creative and delivery decisions within agreed briefs and budgets. Each programme has a named delivery owner. A **Council Convenor** maintains the governance process, with meeting facilitation shared or rotated and no casting vote or unilateral override.

Major direction is developed through consensus-seeking; practical, reviewable policies can use consent. Routine work proceeds through delegated authority. Heart underpins the whole structure through care, participation, respectful disagreement and responsibility towards people and place.

## 1. How the structure works

| Part | Purpose and authority | Boundary |
|---|---|---|
| Shared Stewardship Council | Direction, annual priorities, shared policies, resource-allocation principles and organisational review. | Does not approve every activity or give separate instructions to staff. |
| Working Coordination Group | Connect programmes, community, partnerships and resources; coordinate capacity and resolve issues between teams. | Works within agreed priorities and delegated resources. |
| Programme and working teams | Develop and deliver programmes and everyday services, with autonomy within their mandates. | Escalate matters affecting other teams, exceeding resources or changing agreed direction. |
| Enabling entities | Hold the relevant institutional, financial and contractual responsibilities. | Reserved approvals and delegations must be explicit and aligned with actual entity mandates. |

The six advisors sit within the council as peers. Their advice alone is not an operational instruction. Every shared project still has one named person responsible for following it through.

## 2. Council membership

| Group | Seats | Contribution and selection |
|---|---|---|
| Practice advisors | 6 | Expertise, mentoring and organisational development; appointed against agreed criteria and needs. |
| Programme delegates | 3 | One from each pillar, selected with the relevant programme teams. |
| Heart delegate | 1 | Care, wellbeing and personal growth across Kani, including Heart programmes; selected with people involved in that work. |
| Working-team delegates | 2 | Experience of running the place; selected by the working team. |
| Community representatives | 2 | Local priorities, knowledge and access needs; selected through a process developed with participating communities. |
| Youth representatives | 2 | Young people's perspectives on learning, participation and Kani's future; selected through an accessible youth-led process. |
| **Total** | **16** | One person occupies one seat and has one decision-making position. |

The three programme delegates represent Research, Residencies & Learning; Creative Experiences; and Commercial Experiences & Space Hire. Their remit includes year-round activities beyond Kani Fest. Heart remains the foundation across the pillars, with a dedicated voice in governance.

Representatives consult the people they represent and report back. Council seats are not additional staff posts. Invite specialists for particular questions without automatically adding standing seats.

## 3. The six advisory roles

| Advisory role | Contribution |
|---|---|
| Arts, Culture & Publishing | Creative practice, curatorial mentoring, publishing and cultural relationships. |
| Research & Learning | Residencies, fellowships, learning approaches and research collaborations. |
| Systems & Organisational Development | Governance, decision processes, team structures, documentation and organisational learning. |
| Technology & Digital Practice | Creative technology, digital infrastructure, archives and technical capability. |
| Business & Enterprise | Revenue models, pricing, commercial experiences, hospitality and sustainability. |
| Institutional Partnerships & Fundraising | Institutional networks, grants, CSR, sponsorship and resource mobilisation. |

Each advisor has an agreed contribution and time commitment. Advisors mentor, review and open relationships; named working-role holders carry the continuing work. For example, an advisor may introduce an art school, while the Partnerships Coordinator develops and maintains the collaboration.

## 4. A light Working Coordination Group

Four responsibilities are held across the people actually appointed. One person may hold more than one responsibility only where time, skills, support and workload make this realistic.

| Responsibility | Suggested role title | Year 1 arrangement |
|---|---|---|
| Creative Practice & Learning | Programme Coordinator | Continuing paid role: programme development, creative quality, research, residencies, mentoring and connections across disciplines. |
| Community & Care | Community & Care Coordinator | Defined responsibility with allocated paid time within the team, or part-time support: local relationships, access, participation, feedback and working culture. Works with Heart programme leads. |
| Partnerships & Institutional Development | Partnerships Coordinator | Part-time or retained support: institutional relationships, funding development, proposals and follow-through, supported by relevant advisors. |
| Systems, Operations & Resources | Operations Coordinator | Continuing paid role: shared calendar, resource planning, operational processes, documentation and coordination with accounts, hospitality and technical staff. |

**The initial continuing team is Programme Coordinator, Operations Coordinator and Design & Communications.** Existing accounts and hospitality capacity is additional. Community & Care and Partnerships must have named owners and funded time; they are not assumed to happen through unpaid extra work. Additional support grows with programmes and funding.

Design & Communications leads graphics, audience communication and documentation coordination, working directly with programme owners. Curators, producers, editors, mentors, outreach support and technical specialists are appointed against programme needs and budgets. Systems coordination does not require the Operations Coordinator to personally supply every technical skill.

The Working Coordination Group consists of the people holding these ongoing responsibilities. Its membership need not be four people, and does not automatically expand when a project specialist is engaged. A named commercial booking owner works with Operations and Partnerships within agreed pricing and booking rules.

### Authority and accountability

- Each working role has a written mandate: decisions it can make, consultation requirements, resources, expected contributions and review dates.
- The council appoints and reviews those holding the continuing coordination responsibilities through a defined process involving the working team. Initial role mandates run for one year, with a six-month review; employment or service terms are agreed separately.
- Each person acts within their own mandate. Decisions affecting several functions involve the relevant colleagues and affected programme teams.
- Each staff member has a named support and review contact, avoiding several competing managers.
- Working Coordination Group members attend relevant council discussions. They hold a council decision-making seat only if selected into one of the existing 16 seats; there are no automatic extra seats or duplicate votes.
- When responsibilities are combined in one person, required consultation or approval remains with a distinct authorised colleague. Combining roles must not become self-approval.

## 5. Convening and facilitation

| Role | Responsibility |
|---|---|
| Council Convenor | Gather agenda items, circulate information, organise meetings and follow up on governance commitments. |
| Meeting facilitator | Guide a particular discussion, support equal participation and check agreement explicitly. |
| Working-group Convenor | Organise coordination meetings and follow up on shared operational commitments; an assigned responsibility within the working group. |

The council selects its Convenor through nominations and a consent process for a one-year term, reviewed after six months. Meeting facilitation can rotate among prepared members or be supported externally. Initially, different people convene the council and the working group.

The Convenor has no casting vote, unilateral veto or power to change a collective decision. Every member can submit agenda items. The Convenor cannot exclude a concern without an agreed reason and route for consideration, and represents Kani's position only when authorised. Someone else facilitates matters involving the Convenor's own proposal or interests. Founders are eligible through defined roles; the position is not automatically theirs.

## 6. Who decides what?

| Decision | Decision owner | Required involvement |
|---|---|---|
| Purpose, ethos and major direction | Council | Wider consultation with teams and communities. |
| Annual priorities | Council | Programme proposals and working-group capacity assessment. |
| Annual programme allocations | Council | Accounts and relevant entity confirmation of funds and authorisations. |
| Programme content and artistic selections | Relevant curator or programme team | Consultation appropriate to the agreed brief. |
| Routine spending within an allocation | Authorised budget holder | Agreed financial process and limits. |
| Shared-space or staffing clashes | Affected teams first; working group if unresolved | People responsible for affected work. |
| Routine institutional collaboration | Partnerships Coordinator within delegated authority | Relevant programme and resource leads. |
| Major partnership or long-term commitment | Council and relevant entity | Assessment of value, capacity and obligations. |
| Operational systems and procedures | Relevant function, or working group for shared policies | Staff and users affected by the change. |
| Appointment and review of coordination-role holders | Council | Working-team input; conflicted members step aside. |
| Programme specialist appointments | Relevant team under an agreed process | Budget holder and required entity authorisation. |

Every project has a named delivery owner, including work spanning pillars. Within its agreed brief and resources, a team can proceed without repeated council approval. Advisors, founders and funders use these arrangements rather than giving parallel instructions to staff.

## 7. Reaching agreement

| Decision type | Method |
|---|---|
| Routine work within an agreed mandate | Responsible person decides, consulting affected people. |
| Practical policies and reversible experiments | Consent, with limits and a review date. |
| Major organisational direction | Consensus-seeking, with wider consultation. |
| Immediate operational emergency | Defined emergency authority, followed by review. |

**Consensus** develops a proposal members can support or live with; it need not be everyone's first preference. **Consent** asks whether there is a reasoned objection to trying a proposal within defined limits. It is useful for decisions that can be reviewed and changed. Both require understanding, meaningful participation and an explicit check.

### The decision process

1. **Prepare:** circulate the need, proposal, costs, affected people, decision owner and review date.
2. **Clarify:** answer questions before debating positions.
3. **Hear:** give each eligible participant an opportunity to contribute, with language and access support where needed.
4. **Revise:** address concerns and consider alternatives.
5. **Check:** ask each eligible participant to state their position explicitly.
6. **Record:** document the decision, reservations, owner, resources and next steps.

| Position | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Support | I support the proposal proceeding. |
| Accept with reservations | I can live with it; record my concern. |
| Stand aside | I do not support it, but will not prevent others proceeding; implications for delivery must be clarified. |
| Object | I see a significant problem concerning consequences, feasibility or alignment with Kani's purpose that needs resolving. |

Facilitators help people explain concerns without requiring technical or polished language. **Silence and unanswered messages do not count as agreement.** Standing aside does not imply agreement to provide labour or resources.

### When agreement is not reached

Identify whether the issue is missing information, competing needs, resources, values or a damaged working relationship. Revise the proposal, develop alternatives with those raising concerns, seek independent facilitation, agree a smaller trial, or defer or withdraw it. A deadline does not automatically transfer authority to the Convenor.

A possible voting fallback is approval by three-quarters of eligible council members after a second facilitated discussion, limited to specified decisions. This is a proposal, not an adopted rule: eligible membership, rounding, recusals and the permitted decisions must be defined before use. A result reached this way is recorded as a supermajority decision, not consensus. Until a fallback is agreed, an unresolved proposal remains unresolved.

For a fully occupied 16-seat council, the proposed quorum is **11**, including at least two community/youth representatives and two programme, Heart or working-team delegates. Vacancy, recusal, written-participation and agreement-check rules must be settled before adoption; the six advisors cannot decide alone.

## 8. Budgets, spaces and shared resources

Each programme agreement identifies its purpose, scope, owner, budget, funding conditions, staff time, spaces, equipment, spending authority, participation responsibilities and review dates. Accounts provides an accessible picture of available, committed and spent funds.

| Question | Proposed arrangement |
|---|---|
| Who allocates resources across programmes? | Council, informed by actual funding and team capacity. |
| Who spends within an allocation? | Named authorised budget holder, within written limits. |
| Who agrees transfers between programmes? | Working group within an agreed limit, with affected budget holders; council beyond it, with required entity authorisation. |
| How are commercial and public activities balanced? | Shared calendar and allocation policy, including protected time for public-interest work. |
| How are equipment and spaces maintained? | Transparent booking rules, named maintenance responsibilities and a process for clashes. |

Commercial bookings account for their full demands on staff and facilities. Kani can consider a defined programme fund whose allocations involve community and youth representatives. Institutional relationships, agreements and commitments are documented so continuity does not depend on one person.

## 9. Founders and enabling entities

The proposed roles of SoUF, KiLab and M/s Kani Home must be aligned with their actual mandates and authorisations. A written delegation agreement identifies lead entities, signatories, expenditure powers, team authority, reserved matters and a route for disagreements.

Founders participate through defined responsibilities; any retained approval powers are stated openly. Council agreement alone does not authorise a financial or contractual commitment. Delegations must nevertheless give teams enough authority to carry out approved work without repeatedly seeking fresh permission.

## 10. Participation, care and conflicts of interest

Council appointments run for one year, reviewed annually, with staggered handovers where possible. Agree selection criteria, nomination routes, expected contributions, participation support and a fair replacement process. Community and youth representatives need accessible ways to gather views and report back.

Budget for appropriate honoraria, travel, language and access support. Declare interests in suppliers, applicants, partners and commissions. Members step aside from decisions about their own appointment, fees or connected interests. A replacement or conduct process includes an opportunity to respond.

Heart shapes everyday practice. Every lead remains responsible for respectful working and realistic workloads. Confidential concerns have an impartial route and an alternative contact when the usual lead, founder or Convenor is involved. Sensitive complaints are handled separately from public consensus discussions.

Define emergency authority before launch: who can act, for what situations, within what limits and how promptly decisions are reported and reviewed. Emergency authority is not a general route around agreed governance.

## 11. Working rhythm and records

| Gathering | Proposed rhythm | Focus |
|---|---|---|
| Programme-team check-ins | According to the work | Delivery and immediate decisions. |
| Working Coordination Group | Fortnightly; weekly during busy periods | Shared capacity, resources and issues between teams. |
| Council | Quarterly, with additional decision meetings when needed | Direction, budgets, policy and accountability. |
| Community gathering | Twice yearly | Priorities, feedback and reporting back. |
| Governance review | After six months, then annually | Participation, authority, workload and effectiveness. |

The shared decision record captures what was agreed, why, who participated, who will act and when it will be reviewed. Confidential personnel and complaint records have separate restricted access. Feedback receives a response: what changes, what needs exploration and what cannot proceed, with reasons.

Reviews ask whose contributions shape decisions, who controls information and resources, whether staff can disagree, whether community and youth members influence outcomes, and whether coordination work is recognised and paid.

## 12. Example: an institutional residency

1. The Partnerships Coordinator develops an opportunity with an art or design school.
2. The Programme Coordinator and relevant practitioners develop the brief.
3. The person responsible for Community & Care helps shape local participation, access and support.
4. The Operations Coordinator assesses budget, spaces, staffing and calendar implications.
5. The proposal proceeds within existing delegation, or goes to the council if it introduces a major commitment or changes priorities.
6. The relevant entity authorises the agreement.
7. A named residency lead delivers within the agreed mandate.
8. Participants and partners contribute to a review informing future work.

Where someone holds two functions, these remain distinct checks and consultation involves the affected colleagues. The council sets direction and boundaries, the working functions make collaboration possible, and the programme team carries the work.

## 13. Starting the model

Begin with a six-month pilot after agreeing:

- Founder and entity delegations, financial limits and reserved matters.
- Council selection, representative mandates, participation support and handovers.
- Paid time, staffing and authority for the four coordination responsibilities, with the lean Year 1 arrangements confirmed.
- Quorum, consent and consensus procedures, vacancies, recusals and any voting fallback.
- Convenor selection, independent routes for concerns and emergency arrangements.
- A manageable programme calendar and a review process.

Test the arrangements against a Kani Fest budget, an institutional residency partnership and a commercial booking that clashes with a community programme. The pilot reviews whether authority is shared in practice, decisions sit at the right level, workload is manageable and responsibilities are fulfilled.
