The Advisory Circle brings experience, critical reflection and relationships into Kani without becoming another authority layer. The council governs, the working team runs Kani, programme teams deliver the work, and advisors strengthen all three.

## Six areas of contribution

| Area | Contribution |
|---|---|
| **Arts, Culture & Publishing** | Creative practice, curatorial mentoring, publishing and cultural relationships. |
| **Research & Learning** | Residencies, fellowships, learning methods and research collaborations. |
| **Systems & Organisational Development** | Governance, team structures, documentation and organisational learning. |
| **Technology & Digital Practice** | Creative technology, digital infrastructure, archives and technical capability. |
| **Business & Enterprise** | Revenue models, pricing, commercial experiences, hospitality and sustainability. |
| **Institutional Partnerships & Fundraising** | Institutional networks, grants, CSR, sponsorship and resource mobilisation. |

These are six areas of contribution, not necessarily six separate people. One advisor may cover connected areas where the fit is strong. Kani can invite additional specialists for a particular question without expanding the standing circle.

## What advisors do

Advisors:

- Mentor relevant members of the working and programme teams.
- Review major proposals and annual priorities when invited.
- Build practitioner, institutional, funding and industry relationships.
- Help strengthen systems and organisational capability.
- Join temporary working groups where their expertise is useful.
- Contribute an independent view to the annual review.

Advisors do not manage staff, approve routine spending, direct programme teams or override the council. An introduction or recommendation becomes work only when a named team member accepts responsibility for following it through.

## Appointments and accountability

Each advisor has a one-year appointment stating their area, expected contribution, time commitment, review point and any honorarium. Appointments are reviewed annually and can be renewed.

The circle meets together twice a year. Individual advisors work with relevant people between meetings. They may join council discussions but do not hold a council vote simply by being advisors. If an advisor also holds a council seat or paid role, the two responsibilities and any conflict of interest are recorded separately.

The council records its response to significant advice: accepted, being explored or not proceeding, with a brief reason. This gives advice a clear route into the organisation without turning it into an instruction.
