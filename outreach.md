Participation, Outreach & Community Relationships is a practice that runs across all three pillars and every Kani programme. It is not a separate pillar.

Communications tells people what is happening. Outreach helps people enter the work. Community engagement creates opportunities for people to shape it. Relationship-building allows the connection to continue beyond a single event.

## What are the different functions?

| Function | Primary responsibility |
|---|---|
| **Community engagement** | Building long-term relationships with local communities, practitioners and knowledge holders |
| **Programme outreach** | Bringing relevant participants and contributors into a particular programme |
| **Communications** | Sharing clear public information and helping audiences discover Kani’s work |
| **Institutional partnerships** | Developing relationships with schools, cultural organisations, funders and other collaborators |

These functions support one another, but they are not interchangeable. Public visibility is not the same as community participation, and an institutional partnership does not replace direct relationships with the people affected by a programme.

## How does a relationship develop?

| Stage | What happens |
|---|---|
| **Listen** | Understand existing interests, concerns, knowledge and relationships before defining the programme |
| **Invite and shape** | Identify who should participate and who should help influence the work |
| **Enable participation** | Address barriers involving language, cost, travel, timing, access, confidence or care |
| **Stay connected** | Maintain communication during and after the programme |
| **Respond and learn** | Record what was heard, what changed and who will follow up |

Outreach begins during programme development—not after the programme has already been designed.

## What must every programme do?

Every programme should identify:

- Who it hopes to reach
- Who should help shape the work
- Who holds each relationship
- What barriers may prevent participation
- What access or participation support is required
- What commitments are being made
- How feedback will be received and answered
- How relationships will continue or be respectfully concluded

Relevant outreach costs should be included in the programme budget. These may include visits, translation, travel, access support, facilitation or additional coordination.

## Who carries the work?

The person holding **Community & Care** maintains continuity in Kani’s local and community relationships.

Programme leads remain responsible for the invitations, promises and relationships created through their programmes.

**Design & Communications** makes public information understandable and accessible.

The **Partnerships lead** maintains institutional, educational, funding and strategic relationships.

Busy or geographically distributed programmes may appoint additional outreach capacity. Volunteer coordination remains a separate delivery responsibility within Kanifest and other large programmes.

## How are relationships recorded responsibly?

Kani maintains a shared internal record of interests, commitments and follow-ups. Personal contact details are stored only with appropriate consent and access.

The record should help the team remember relationships; it should not reduce people to an audience or marketing list.

## How do we know outreach is meaningful?

We ask whether:

- Relevant people were involved early enough to influence the work
- Participation extended beyond the people already closest to Kani
- Barriers were recognised and addressed
- Commitments were followed through
- Feedback resulted in a response or visible change
- Relationships can continue beyond a single programme
