## What is the Kani Council?

The Kani Council holds responsibility for Kani’s shared direction and accountability. It connects people doing the work with community perspectives and the legal, financial and institutional stewardship of the place.

Council membership begins with seven seats and may expand to nine when a review identifies a clear gap in representation.

### Council membership

| Members | Seats | Contribution |
|---|---:|---|
| Working Team representatives | 2 | Programme knowledge, operations, capacity and everyday working conditions |
| Community representatives | 2 | Local knowledge, participation, access and community priorities |
| Youth representative | 1 | Young people’s perspectives on learning, participation and Kani’s future |
| Founding and enabling-entity representatives | 2 | Institutional continuity, legal and financial responsibilities, and stewardship of the place |
| Independent public-interest or practitioner representatives | 0–2 | Additional perspectives where a specific gap in representation or accountability has been identified |
| **Total** | **7–9** | Members share responsibility for Kani as a whole |

Additional seats should not be created simply to include more expertise. Expertise can enter through the Advisory Circle. A Council seat is appropriate when someone needs to share continuing governance responsibility.

Council seats are not additional staff positions or honorary titles. Members must be able to prepare for decisions, represent perspectives beyond themselves, declare conflicts and remain accountable for their participation.

Council roles may be unpaid, but participation time, honoraria, travel and access support should be agreed wherever possible.

### What does the Council do?

The Council:

- Protects Kani’s purpose and ethos
- Agrees annual priorities and overall budget allocations
- Reviews the balance of work across the three pillars
- Approves major partnerships, new platforms and long-term commitments
- Reviews programme outcomes, finances and working conditions
- Establishes clear responsibilities and decision boundaries
- Oversees fair appointment and review processes
- Reviews whether Kani’s governance and selection processes are working

The Council does not select every artist, approve every purchase or issue routine instructions to the Working Team.

One member serves as **Council Convenor** for a limited term. The Convenor gathers agenda items, circulates information and follows up on decisions. Facilitation may rotate. The Convenor has no casting vote or unilateral authority.

## What is the Advisory Circle?

The Advisory Circle brings experience, reflection and relationships into Kani without becoming another authority layer.

It can be broader and more flexible than the Council because advisors do not carry governance votes or organisational decision-making authority.

| Area | Contribution |
|---|---|
| **Arts, Culture & Publishing** | Creative practice, curatorial mentoring, publishing and cultural relationships |
| **Research & Learning** | Residencies, fellowships, research collaborations and learning methods |
| **Systems & Organisational Development** | Governance, documentation, team structures and organisational learning |
| **Technology & Digital Practice** | Creative technology, digital infrastructure, archives and technical capability |
| **Business & Enterprise** | Revenue models, pricing, commercial experiences, hospitality and sustainability |
| **Institutional Partnerships & Fundraising** | Institutional networks, grants, CSR, sponsorship and resource mobilisation |

These are six areas of contribution, not necessarily six separate people. One area may include more than one advisor, and one advisor may contribute across connected areas.

Additional specialists may be invited for a particular question without creating new Council seats or permanent appointments.

Advisors may:

- Mentor members of the Working Team and Programme Teams
- Review proposals when their expertise is relevant
- Strengthen practitioner, institutional, funding and industry relationships
- Support organisational learning and capability
- Contribute an independent view to periodic reviews

Advisors do not:

- Hold a governance vote through their advisory appointment
- Manage staff
- Approve routine spending
- Direct Programme Teams
- Automatically join selection panels
- Make commitments on behalf of Kani

An introduction or recommendation becomes work only when a named Working Team member accepts responsibility for it. If an advisor recommends a person or organisation being considered, the relationship is declared.

## What is the Working Team?

The Working Team maintains continuity across Kani’s programmes and keeps the place functioning throughout the year.

### Continuing roles

| Role | Main responsibility |
|---|---|
| **Programme Coordinator** | Maintains the programme pipeline, convenes appropriate reviews, connects initiatives, supports programme leads and carries learning between programmes |
| **Operations Coordinator** | Maintains the shared calendar, resource plan, venue readiness, working systems and coordination with Accounts, Hospitality and technical support |
| **Design & Communications** | Maintains Kani’s visual identity, website, printed and digital communication, confirmed programme information and documentation standards |
| **Social Media & Audience Coordinator** | Maintains the social media calendar and public-facing digital presence; coordinates programme campaigns, audience enquiries, content assets, consent and engagement review |

The Programme Coordinator holds the selection process but does not approve every programme alone.

### Allocated or retained responsibilities

| Responsibility | Starting arrangement |
|---|---|
| **Partnerships, Funding & Commercial Development** | A named part-time or retained person maintains institutional and client relationships, develops funding and commercial opportunities, supports proposals and holds reporting commitments |
| **Community & Care** | Allocated paid time within a suitable role, or part-time support, for community relationships, participation, access, feedback and practical care |

These responsibilities require named capacity even when they are not separate full-time positions.

The Working Team meets regularly to review:

- Programme proposals and upcoming commitments
- Capacity and workload
- Space, equipment and scheduling conflicts
- Partnerships, funding and commercial enquiries
- Community relationships and access requirements
- Public communication, audience activity and upcoming campaigns
- Decisions that may require Council involvement

Coordination does not make one person the head of the team. Each role has a written mandate and a named support and review contact.

## What are Programme Teams?

Programme Teams are temporary or continuing groups formed to develop and deliver approved programmes.

A Programme Team is created only after the programme has:

- Been reviewed and approved
- Received a clear brief
- Identified its creative and delivery authority
- Secured an appropriate budget and resource plan
- Established its participation, care and review requirements

A small programme may have one person holding both creative and delivery responsibility. Larger programmes should separate these roles.

Possible appointments include:

- Curator or creative lead
- Producer or delivery lead
- Researcher, mentor or facilitator
- Community or participant liaison
- Editor or publication lead
- Technical and production specialists
- Artists, residents, fellows or contributors
- Volunteer coordinator for programmes using volunteer teams

Programme appointments do not automatically create Council seats or authority beyond the approved brief.

At the end of a programme, its team hands over relevant relationships, records, financial information, documentation and learning to the appropriate continuing Working Team member.

## How do the different parts relate?

- The **Advisory Circle** contributes expertise and relationships.
- The **Council** holds direction and accountability.
- The **Working Team** maintains continuity and shared systems.
- **Programme Teams** hold creative and delivery authority for particular work.

A person may hold more than one role, but the responsibilities, decision authority and conflicts associated with each role must remain clear.
