Named contacts and detailed review arrangements must be established before this becomes an operating process.

Care is a shared responsibility across Kani. It includes respectful working, realistic workloads, fair compensation, accessible participation, attention to consent and a safe route for raising concerns.

Disagreement is part of working together. Kani aims to address it early and fairly without making people fear the loss of work, access or belonging.

## What kinds of concerns may arise?

| Type of concern | Starting route |
|---|---|
| **Creative or practical disagreement** | The people directly responsible discuss it against the approved brief and available resources |
| **Conflict over spaces, time, budgets or responsibilities** | Relevant Working Team members clarify commitments and help develop an agreement |
| **Concern about a selection or appointment process** | A non-conflicted person reviews whether the stated process and criteria were followed |
| **Unresolved disagreement affecting Kani’s direction or policy** | The Council considers it through a facilitated discussion |
| **Concern about conduct, harassment, discrimination, retaliation or misuse of authority** | A confidential route involving an impartial contact |
| **Concern involving a Council member, founder or usual contact** | An alternative contact outside the immediate relationship |

Different concerns require different responses. Serious conduct concerns are not treated as ordinary creative disagreements.

## How is a working disagreement addressed?

First clarify what is disputed:

- The goal or intended outcome
- Decision authority
- Resources or workload
- Behaviour or communication
- An unmet commitment
- The interpretation of an agreed brief

The people involved explain what they need and consider workable options. A colleague may facilitate when everyone is comfortable with that arrangement.

If the matter remains unresolved, it goes to the appropriate Working Team responsibility or, where it exceeds their mandate, the Council.

A person directly involved in the disagreement does not act as its impartial facilitator.

Any agreement records what will happen, who is responsible and when it will be reviewed.

## What if someone questions a selection or appointment?

A person may raise a concern when they believe:

- The published process was not followed
- Criteria changed without explanation
- A conflict of interest was not declared
- Relevant information was ignored
- Discrimination or retaliation affected the outcome
- A person acted beyond their appointment authority

The review examines the fairness and integrity of the process. It does not automatically replace a curatorial or professional judgement merely because someone disagrees with the result.

A review may:

- Confirm that the process was followed
- Require an explanation or correction
- Reopen part or all of the process
- Appoint new non-conflicted decision-makers
- Recommend changes for future selections

Sensitive applicant and appointment information remains private.

## How can someone raise a sensitive concern?

Kani names:

- A confidential contact
- An alternative contact outside the immediate working relationship
- A route to independent or external support when required

People may approach the alternative directly when a concern involves their usual contact, a programme lead, founder, Council member or someone with authority over their work.

No one is required to confront the person concerned before seeking support. Mediation is voluntary and is not assumed to be appropriate for serious misconduct.

Before information is shared, the person raising the concern is told:

- Who may need access
- Why the information is needed
- What can and cannot remain confidential
- What the next step may be

Sensitive concerns are not placed in public meeting minutes.

## What should the response provide?

The process should provide:

- Prompt acknowledgement
- A clear next step and approximate timeframe
- Practical support where needed
- Protection against retaliation
- An impartial assessment
- A fair opportunity for relevant people to be heard
- A reasoned outcome
- An independent route to request review

Immediate protective action may be taken when necessary for safety or to prevent retaliation. Such action is not itself a final finding.

## How are conflicts of interest handled?

Anyone connected to an applicant, supplier, partner, disputed decision or person involved in a concern declares the relationship.

A conflicted person may provide factual information but does not control the decision or review.

If conflicts leave too few eligible people internally, Kani seeks an appropriate external reviewer.

## How are people in positions of authority held accountable?

Concerns about a Council member, advisor, Working Team member or Programme Team lead are reviewed by non-conflicted people with authority appropriate to the role.

The review considers:

- The person’s written mandate
- The seriousness and pattern of the concern
- Their explanation and available evidence
- Harm or risk to other people and Kani
- Whether unclear roles, poor information or unrealistic workloads contributed
- What corrective, protective or organisational action is required

Possible outcomes include clarification, support, changed responsibilities, a formal warning, facilitated repair, review of the appointment or conclusion of the role.

Any replacement follows [Appointments, Terms & Review](#structure-appointments).

## Can people use an external route?

Yes. People may use appropriate legal, professional, safeguarding or complaint routes without first exhausting Kani’s internal process.

Kani should not prevent someone from seeking independent advice or support.
